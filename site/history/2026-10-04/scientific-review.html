Uncertainty and limitations

  • Freshness, missingness, measurement method, spatial representativeness and source independence constrain interpretation.
  • Model, satellite, inventory, regulatory, traffic and media records are different evidence classes and are not interchangeable with ground measurements.
  • A technical join demonstrates record linkage only; it does not demonstrate contribution or causation.

Questions for scientific review

  1. Are the selected measurements fresh, quality-eligible and spatially representative for the stated geography?
  2. Were comparator roles, averaging periods, units and measurement methods shown to be compatible?
  3. Were meteorology and competing-source evidence aligned to the same time window?
  4. Which joins were executed against real records, and what were their match and duplicate rates?
  5. Which plausible alternative explanations remain untested?

These questions are intended to make the output challengeable and reproducible rather than persuasive.

Scientific and legal boundary

Screening evidence only — no causal source attribution, legal or regulatory determination, health finding, safety declaration or compliance conclusion.

Named facilities, operators, permits, roads, fires and planning records are contextual evidence. Inclusion or proximity never implies contribution, fault, breach or causation.

Operational PM2.5 screen: threshold and multiplicity boundary

The current screen uses operational screening parameters including a 24-hour PM2.5 mean floor of 15 µg/m³ and a robust station-history score of 3 or greater. These are project screening parameters, not UK legal limits, WHO guideline values, health thresholds, or evidence of a pollution source.

Calibration status. Until an empirical null, sensitivity analysis and dependence-aware calibration are published, the project does not claim that the nominal Benjamini–Hochberg alpha establishes a validated false-discovery rate. The scores are screening statistics rather than calibrated p-values.

WHO context is not UK legal compliance

For NO₂, the WHO 2021 annual air-quality guideline is 10 µg/m³, while the UK statutory annual-mean limit value/objective is 40 µg/m³. WHO-context pollutant-day counts must not be interpreted as UK legal exceedance counts.
